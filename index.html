What this page is

Every evening a GitHub Action saves the Southwest PSAs from the national 7-Day Significant Fire Potential (all seven days) to data/forecasts.csv. It then checks the previous day's observed weather against the Wind and Burn Environment criteria in use that day and saves the result to data/observed.csv. Lightning is confirmed by hand.

Forecast categories

How observed High Risk is decided

Each RAWS station is checked on its own. A PSA meets a trigger when enough stations hit it, not the PSA average.

  • Wind (W): an hour with sustained wind at or above the limit and RH at or below the limit, or several hours of gusts at or above the gust limit with low RH.
  • Burn Environment (B): afternoon max temperature well above the typical peak-season max with low RH, plus elevated instability (high-elevation Haines from the nearest 00Z sounding). Typical peak-season max is estimated from station elevation unless a PSA value is set. B? means the station part was met but no sounding was available.
  • Lightning (L): confirmed by hand. Add a row to data/lightning.csv for each PSA-day you confirm. The Weather check tab lists Lightning forecasts waiting for review with the number of dry stations as context.

Changing the criteria

Use the Criteria tab. You can keep several named sets (for example “Standard” and “Hot season”) and switch which one is in use, starting on any date you choose. Each day is checked with the set that was in use that day, so earlier results keep their original criteria unless you edit that set.

Saving opens GitHub with your changes filled in; click Submit new issue there. An automatic step checks the numbers, saves them, re-checks past days, and replies on that issue. The site updates within a few minutes.

The Little/Low/Moderate risk levels depend on fuels (ERC and other indices), so the weather check covers the High Risk triggers only.

Scores

  • POD: share of event days that were forecast “yes”. Higher is better.
  • FAR: share of “yes” forecasts with no event. Lower is better.
  • CSI: hits ÷ (hits + misses + false alarms). Higher is better.
  • Bias: “yes” forecasts ÷ event days. Above 1 means over-forecasting.
